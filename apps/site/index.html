    Why two numbers?

    Same NEA data. Two clocks.

    Both are from NEA. The 24-hr PSI averages the last 24 hours, so it changes slowly and is best for planning tomorrow. The 1-hr PM2.5 shows the last hour, and NEA recommends it for deciding what to do right now.

    Why open source

    A health tool you can check.

    • Free, for good

      No premium tier. Nothing held back during a haze.

    • Never tracked

      No ads, no accounts, no cookies. We only count totals, like visits, downloads and shares, never who. Alerts are optional: we keep only a push address, your area and who you're checking for, and turning them off deletes it all.

    • Your location stays on your phone

      It only picks your nearest NEA station, on the device.

    • Official data, every line public

      NEA's numbers via data.gov.sg (elsewhere, each country's authority), never changed. MIT licensed, one spec for every app.

    About HazeNow

    I built HazeNow because the number most of us check during a haze, the 24-hr PSI, moves slowly. NEA also publishes the last hour's PM2.5, and recommends it for deciding what to do right now. HazeNow puts that number first, in plain words, using only NEA's data.

    It's free and open source (MIT). No ads, no accounts, and we never track you. We only count things in total, like visits, downloads and shares — never who did them. Your location stays on your phone.

    — Yong Quan Tan

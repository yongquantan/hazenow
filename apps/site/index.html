How it looks

The verdict first. The number second.

Every HazeNow screen follows the same order: plain advice, NEA's 1-hr PM2.5 and band, where it was measured, then NEA's 24-hr PSI. People act on a sentence, not on a number alone.

  • Where it was measured, on the main screen. Station, distance and how long ago.
  • Honest ranges. When your spot is between stations, we show what nearby stations read.
  • Calm by design. NEA's band names and colours, a shape for every band, no alarm red for Elevated.

Why two numbers?

Same NEA data. Two clocks.

NEA's 24-hr PSI is an average of the last 24 hours, so it changes slowly and is best for planning tomorrow. The 1-hr PM2.5 shows the last hour, and NEA recommends it for deciding what to do right now.

The chart shows it instead of saying it: hourly bars for the 1-hr PM2.5, and NEA's own 24-hour average as a line, both in µg/m³. No invented index.

Built to be shared

Made for the group chat.

Haze news travels through family, class and running chats. One tap picks the right card. Each one prints the date, time, area and "Data: NEA via data.gov.sg", so a forwarded image stays honest.

  • Now

    For family and neighbourhood chats: NEA's own advice for most people and for vulnerable groups.

  • Two clocks

    For anyone asking why the numbers differ. The chart explains it without taking sides.

  • For our group

    For a parent group, a run club or a caregiver chat. Advice for one group, said plainly.

  • All clear

    For the same chats, when the air is back to Normal. Relief is worth sharing too.

Why open source

A health tool you can check.

Trust is the whole point of HazeNow, so everything it does is public.

  • Free, for good

    No price, no premium tier, no "pro" features held back during a haze.

  • No ads, no tracking, no account

    No analytics, no sign-up, nothing sold. Profiles are saved on your device only.

  • Your location stays on your phone

    We use it only to pick the nearest NEA stations, on the device. It's never sent anywhere.

  • Official data, credited

    In Singapore, every number is NEA's, via data.gov.sg. Elsewhere, each country's own authority. We don't change readings or invent an index.

  • Audit every line

    MIT licensed. The maths, the wording and the advice rules are all in the repository.

  • One set of rules, every app

    Web, phone, Mac and integrations share one written spec and test cases, so they agree.

Southeast Asia

The same calm answer, across the region.

Each place uses its own authority's numbers and its own band words. Thailand is live on the web. Other countries are built and tested on recorded data, and go live once our data server is up and each authority agrees.

Haze has two seasons here: the southern one from Jul to Oct, and the northern one from Jan to Apr. HazeNow is built to be ready for both.

About HazeNow

I built HazeNow because the number most of us check during a haze, the 24-hr PSI, moves slowly. NEA also publishes the last hour's PM2.5, and recommends it for deciding what to do right now. HazeNow puts that number first, in plain words, using only NEA's data.

It's free and open source (MIT). No ads, no tracking, no account. Your location stays on your phone.

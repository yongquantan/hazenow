# HazeNow: relevance calendar, rollout priority, yearly playbook

**Goal:** be the calm, trusted tool people already have installed, or find on day one, whenever haze starts anywhere in Southeast Asia, every year.

This doc answers three questions: *when* people need us (seasonality and search demand), *where* we can show up credibly (relevance × data feasibility), and *what we do each month* so we're top of mind before an episode starts, not after.

Researched **2026-09-28**, during the live southern-ASEAN episode.

**Evidence rules**
- Every factual claim has a link. `(search)` means the claim was seen in search-result text and the page was not opened.
- *unverified* means it was not confirmed. *inferred* means it's our synthesis, not a source.
- Search-demand numbers come from Google Trends series we downloaded ourselves today (method in §3). They are not taken from articles.
- The research budget ran out before we could check Google "Year in Search" lists, app-download spikes (AirVisual etc.) and GDELT news volume. GDELT rate-limited every call. **Nothing here relies on those three.**

Related docs: [sea/REGIONAL.md](sea/REGIONAL.md) (feeds, ASMC, FIRMS, architecture) · [DISTRIBUTION.md](DISTRIBUTION.md) · [SHARING.md](SHARING.md) · country docs in [sea/](sea/). There is no `COVERAGE.md` yet, so feasibility below comes from REGIONAL.md §1.3 and each country doc's **Verdict**.

## 0. Headline

**SEA has two haze clocks and one urban-smog clock.** They cover almost the whole year:

| system | months (typical) | who | what drives bad years |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Southern ASEAN transboundary haze** (Sumatra + Kalimantan peat/forest fires, carried by Southwest Monsoon winds) | **Jul–Oct**, peak **Sep**; a minor Riau season in **Feb–Mar** | SG, MY (Peninsula + Sarawak), ID fire belt, BN, S. Thailand, and the Visayas in big years | **El Niño + positive IOD**: 1997, 2006, 2015, 2019, 2023, 2026 |
| **Northern Mekong burning season** (agricultural + forest burning, basin inversions) | **Jan–Apr**, peak **Mar** | N. Thailand (Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai), Laos, Myanmar, Cambodia, N. Vietnam | dry years; 2019, 2023, 2024, 2026 were bad |
| **Urban winter smog** (traffic + inversions + regional burning) | **Nov/Dec–Feb/Mar**, peak **Jan** | Bangkok, Hanoi, (HCMC, Phnom Penh, Manila, milder) | still, dry winters; 2019, 2025 Bangkok school closures |

The calendar therefore runs: **Nov–Mar = Thailand/Mekong/Hanoi**, **Jul–Oct = Malaysia/Indonesia/Singapore/Brunei**. The gaps (Apr–Jun, Nov) are the outlook-and-recap months.

**Search demand is spiky.** In Singapore, searches for "haze" sit at zero for most of the year (145 of 262 weeks were 0) and jump to ~240× baseline within 1–2 days of the PSI rising. They decay within 2–4 days. **You can't be discovered during the spike. You have to be installed before it.** That's why §5 (retention) and §6 (early warning) matter more than any launch.

## 1. Seasonality, verified per country and city

### 1.1 The southern ASEAN transboundary haze

**Season mechanics**
- The southern "traditional dry season" matches the **Southwest Monsoon, Jun–Sep/early Oct**. South-easterly to south-westerly winds carry Sumatra and Kalimantan smoke to Singapore and Malaysia. The inter-monsoon months (Apr–May, Oct–Nov) bring light, variable winds and more rain. In El Niño years the dry season can run into October ([MSS](https://www.weather.gov.sg/drier-and-warmer-southwest-monsoon-season/), [MSS climate](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/) (search)). Wikipedia's summary: haze is "generally worst between July and October and during El Niño events" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_haze)).
- **Two fire peaks in Riau/north Sumatra: Feb–Mar and Jul–Aug.** Kalimantan has one, **Jul–Oct** ([Mongabay 2024](https://news.mongabay.com/2024/03/sumatra-firefighters-on-alert-as-burning-heralds-start-of-riau-dry-season/) (search); [Pantau Gambut](https://en.pantaugambut.id/publications/the-ring-of-fire-1-land-and-forest-fires-in-riau) (search); [Remote Sensing 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12010005) (search)).

**The El Niño / positive IOD link**
- Indonesia's six major fire years in 1979–2016 were linked to a **positive Indian Ocean Dipole**. 1997–98 combined the century's strongest El Niño with a +IOD, and 2006 was a moderate El Niño plus a +IOD ([NASA ESPO](https://espo.nasa.gov/s-mode/content/Connecting_Indonesian_Fires_and_Drought_With_the_Type_of_El_Ni%C3%B1o_and_Phase_of_the_Indian) (search)).
- In Sep–Oct 2015, El Niño plus a +IOD produced Equatorial Asia's largest fire CO₂ emissions since 1997 ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1524888113) (search)).
- Peat groundwater dropped 1.0–1.5 m in 1997/98, 2015 and 2019 ([Sci. Rep. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-27393-x) (search)).
- Three-month rainfall explains ~79% of the variance in fire emissions. Severe burning starts below ~350 mm over 4 months in south Sumatra and ~650 mm in south Kalimantan ([Field & Shen 2008, JGR](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2008JG000694)).
- So: **a developing El Niño by May–June, plus a positive IOD forecast, is the single best sign of a bad southern season.**

**Pattern:** the southern haze is a boom-bust system. It's severe roughly 1 year in 3–4, tied to El Niño/+IOD. **The Mekong/urban season is bad almost every year.** For a product that must "own relevance every year", the north is the reliable annual audience, and the south is the big-spike audience.

## 3. Search demand: when people look for a tool

**Method:** Google Trends. We called `trends.google.com/trends/api/explore` to get a TIMESERIES token, then `/api/widgetdata/multiline` with a browser UA and cookie, tz −480.
- **Windows pulled:** 5-year weekly (Sep 2021–Sep 2026), all-time monthly from 2004, and 90-day daily.
- **Scaling:** each series is scaled 0–100 **within itself**, so values can't be compared across countries or terms.
- **"Baseline"** is the mean of the lowest 90% of weeks. The **peak/base** ratio is 100 divided by that baseline.
- **Distortion:** the Sep 2026 episode is the peak of most southern 5-year series, which flattens earlier years.
- Raw JSON and scripts are in the session scratchpad; they aren't committed.

**Implications**
1. **SG, BN and PH are "flash" markets.** Demand is near zero until the day the air turns, then gone within a week. Only installed users, or a page already ranking for "haze today", catch it.
2. **MY and ID are "season" markets.** Interest stays high for 4–6 weeks. A Malaysian launch in mid-August would still catch most of the demand; a Singapore launch in mid-September would miss most of it.
3. **TH is the most reliable annual market.** Every January, demand runs 28× baseline, with a November onset. Plus Chiang Mai in March.
4. **VN is a habit market** with a high baseline. Daily-use features (widgets, commute checks) matter more there than spike marketing.

### 5.4 Stay installed between seasons: retention mechanics

The risk: in a quiet year (2024 in the south) a user opens HazeNow zero times, and the app becomes a candidate for deletion. The goal is **earned, near-silent presence**, not engagement. No streaks, no daily nags, no "we miss you".

1. **Season bookends (opt-in, ≤ 4 messages per system per year outside episodes).** Outlook, season start, stand-down, recap. Each message is useful even to someone who never saw haze that year ("Season's over for 2026. 0 hours above Normal where you are. See you next year."). **This is the #1 relevance mechanic** (§7).
2. **The all-clear, always** (SPEC §5, UX_PSYCHOLOGY R6). Relief messages reset alert fatigue, so they're the reason people keep notifications on.
3. **An ambient widget that's pleasant when the air is clean.** The widget, menu-bar and Scriptable surfaces show "Normal · 12 µg/m³ · haze season: quiet (ASMC L0)" all year. It's a zero-cost reminder that the tool exists, and a place for the season status line.
4. **Multi-season value for the same user.** Saved places across borders (REGIONAL §3.5): an SG user who travels to Chiang Mai in March, or a JB commuter, gets a second season from the same install. **Every country launch raises retention for existing users**, not just acquisition.
5. **The Telegram bot is a dormant subscription.** It costs nothing between seasons, and the first-rise alert arrives through a channel that's already trusted. Its `/season` command returns the status line and the next expected milestone.
6. **Evergreen "haze today" pages per city,** indexed before the season, that match real queries ("haze level now", "jerebu hari ini", "cek ipu jerebu", "brunei haze index today"). **This is how the flash-market spike finds us** when the user isn't installed. Programmatic pages built from the same edge JSON, in the local language.
7. **Recap as a shareable object.** An "Our 2026 haze season" card (region-level, public data, no personal data). SHARING.md's All-clear and Now card templates extend to it. It gives trusted messengers a reason to post in November.
8. **Off-season contributor loop.** Translation and data-source issues (Thai, Malay, Indonesian, Vietnamese copy; country adapters) posted as "good first issues" in the quiet months. Contributors become local messengers when the season arrives.
9. **Optional calendar subscription** (`.ics`) of season milestones, with dates filled in when ASMC announces them. It's low effort and useful to schools and sports clubs planning outdoor events. *Idea, untested.*

**Don'ts:** no off-season pushes beyond the bookends; no "air quality score" gamification; no daily "check-in" prompts; no purifier or mask commerce (DISTRIBUTION §7).

### 5.5 Early-warning spec: "Season Watch" (minimal)

**Purpose:** let HazeNow say something useful *before* PM2.5 rises, using free leading indicators, without predicting a number and without alarm. It is context, never a verdict. The band and verdict still come only from the authority's measured PM2.5 (REGIONAL §3.3).

#### 5.5.1 States (per user place, per system: southern / Mekong)

| state | enters when | leaves when | what the user sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Off-season** | ASMC L0 for the sub-region, or outside the season window with no alert | L1 detected | nothing, except the widget/settings line "Haze season: quiet" |
| **Outlook** (once, pre-season) | calendar: 1–15 Jun (south), 1–15 Nov (north), **and** a CPC/BOM status is available | after one showing | one card: *"Climate outlook: NOAA reports El Niño conditions, and BOM's Indian Ocean index is positive. In past years this has meant a drier fire season in the region. Nothing to do now. We'll let you know if the air changes."* Links: CPC, BOM, SIIA/ASMC. Neutral years get neutral copy: *"No El Niño this year (NOAA). Haze season usually starts around July."* |
| **Season open** | ASMC L1 detected (RSS/alerts page) | L0, or escalation | one line, shown once: *"Haze season has started for our region (ASMC, 10 Jul). Nothing to do today."* |
| **Watch** | **(A)** ASMC L2/L3 for the sub-region **or** **(B)** our FIRMS count in a source box ≥ threshold on 2 of the last 3 days, **and** forecast wind at the user's place blows from that box for ≥ 12 of the next 48 h | measured PM2.5 band ≥ Elevated (hand over to Live), **or** 5 days without (A)/(B) | card: *"More fires than usual in Sumatra, and winds over the next two days blow from there towards Singapore. The air here is Normal right now. We'll tell you if it changes."* Plus FIRMS/ASMC links. **Optional single push per Watch entry**, opt-in only, outside quiet hours |
| **Live** | band ≥ Elevated (existing product) | band Normal for 12 h → all-clear | the existing verdict. Season Watch shrinks to one context line ("Fires upwind: 163 hotspots in Kalimantan, NASA FIRMS") per REGIONAL §2.2 |
| **Stand-down** | ASMC L0 (or 21 days quiet plus inter-monsoon) | – | season recap card (§5.4 #7) |

#### 5.5.2 Inputs (all free)

| input | source | fetched by | cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| ENSO status | CPC `ensodisc.shtml` status line + `RONI.ascii.txt` | edge | daily check, changes monthly |
| IOD | BOM `iod_1.txt` | edge | weekly |
| ASMC level + L1/L0 events | `asmc.asean.org/feed/` + `/asmc-alerts/` | edge (detect only; show as a **link** until MSS permission, REGIONAL §2.1) | every 6 h |
| Hotspot counts | FIRMS keyless `J1_VIIRS_C2_SouthEast_Asia_24h.csv` (+ J2), `confidence=h` or `n` with FRP > 5 MW | edge | every 30 min, `If-Modified-Since` |
| Wind forecast | Open-Meteo `wind_direction_10m` (+ 850 hPa) at geohash-3 | **client** (CORS `*`) or edge per tile | hourly |
| CAMS PM2.5 trend | Open-Meteo Air Quality | edge, internal only | 6-hourly. Used only to *suppress* a Watch when CAMS shows no rise at the user's tile; **never displayed** |

**Source boxes (v0):** south Sumatra/Jambi, Riau, West Kalimantan, Central/South Kalimantan, Peninsular Malaysia, Sarawak; Mekong north (N. Thailand + N. Laos + Shan State), Cambodia/S. Laos. The edge publishes `/v3/context/season.json`: `{system, asmcLevel, asmcSince, asmcUrl, enso, iod, boxes:[{id, count24h[3], threshold, over}], updatedAt}`.

**Privacy:** the client combines the box counts with its own wind lookup. No location reaches the edge (REGIONAL §4.1).

#### 5.5.3 Thresholds (provisional; backtest before shipping)
- **South:** Kalimantan box ≥ 150/day, Sumatra box ≥ 50/day (high-confidence NOAA-20). These sit just below ASMC's L2/L3 trigger counts (185/371 and 287/190 Kalimantan; 65/81 Sumatra).
- **Mekong:** Mekong-north box ≥ 500/day (ASMC L2 counts were 608/531).
- **Wind match:** direction within ±45° of the bearing from the box centroid to the user, speed > 2 m/s.
- **Backtest:** replay 2023, 2025 and 2026 (FIRMS archive via a free MAP_KEY, NEA/Air4Thai history) and target **Watch preceding ≥ 70% of Elevated onsets by ≥ 12 h, with ≤ 1 false Watch per month.** If it can't meet that, ship **(A) ASMC-only Watch** and drop (B).

#### 5.5.4 Copy rules
- Say what is observed: fires counted, wind direction, an official level. Never a predicted PM2.5, never a date ("haze will arrive Thursday"), never a colour stronger than neutral. Watch uses the Normal palette with an outline icon.
- Always pair it with "the air here is [band] right now" and "nothing to do now", or with one preparatory action for sensitive profiles only ("If you have asthma, check your inhaler is with you.").
- Attribution is always visible: "NASA FIRMS", "ASMC", "NOAA", "BOM".
- Don't attribute cause or blame ("fires in Indonesia caused…"). Say where the fires are, not whose they are.
- Languages: the same `COPY.<cc>.md` pipeline as the verdicts.

#### 5.5.5 Surfaces and limits
- **Surfaces:** a card on the home screen, one line on the widget, the bot's `/season` command, and the evergreen city page (the "Season: Watch" chip helps SEO too).
- **Push limits:** ≤ 1 Watch push per episode entry; ≤ 4 bookend messages per system per year; the existing 3/day cap; the all-clear always.

#### 5.5.6 Non-goals (v0)
- No PM2.5 forecast number.
- No FWI or drought maps.
- No Himawari (licence).
- No per-user ML.

**Build order:** after the Thai launch. Target **1 Jun 2027** for the south, with the Mekong version in **Dec 2027** (or Dec 2026 as ASMC-only if time allows).

## 7. Summary

**Calendar headline:** Southeast Asia is never haze-free. It runs on two haze clocks and one smog clock.
- **Nov–Apr, the north:** Bangkok peaks in Jan, Chiang Mai and Laos in Mar, Hanoi in Dec–Jan. Thai "pm2.5" searches hit ~28× baseline every January.
- **Jul–Oct, the south:** the Sumatra/Kalimantan peat season peaks in Sep. It's severe in El Niño/+IOD years: 1997, 2015, 2019, and now 2026 (ASMC Level 3 since 26 Aug, possibly a historic El Niño).
- **Search is spiky:** SG, BN and PH jump ~240× in a day and fade in 2–4 days. MY and ID stay high for 4–6 weeks, starting 3–4 weeks before SG.

**Top 3 rollout priorities**
1. **Thailand.** Bangkok **before 15 Nov 2026**, and the North plus Laos **before 15 Jan 2027**. It has the only "ship now" data (Air4Thai 1-hr, CORS-open) and is relevant every year. The blocker is the PCD licence.
2. **Malaysia.** Klang Valley, JB and Sarawak **before 1 Jun 2027**. It gets haze earliest and longest in the south, and it covers JB ↔ SG commuters. Ask DOE for permission in Oct 2026.
3. **Indonesia.** The fire-belt cities and Jakarta **before 1 Jul 2027**, with a Riau pilot by 1 Feb 2027 if BMKG agrees. The stakes are highest here, and permissions are the critical path.

**#1 relevance mechanic: Season bookends plus Season Watch.** For each system, HazeNow sends an opt-in, calm, cited message at a few moments:
- the **outlook** (NOAA/BOM + SIIA/ASMC);
- **season start** (ASMC Level 1);
- one **Watch** when fires upwind and the wind line up;
- the **all-clear**;
- a shareable **recap**.

Outside episodes that's four or fewer quiet messages a year. HazeNow stays installed and trusted, so it speaks first when the next episode starts.
